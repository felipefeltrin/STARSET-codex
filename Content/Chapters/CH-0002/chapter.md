## Abstract

This chapter presents the known history of the STARSET narrative as a continuous story, beginning with the future that produced the PROX warning and ending at the present narrative frontier represented by *SILOS*. It integrates the two primary novels, the Marvel adaptation, the first five studio albums, official visual media, and the 2024 *IMMERSION: THE FINAL CHAPTER* demonstrations.

The chronology is intentionally conservative. STARSET's story is distributed across media that do not always supply exact dates or a single explicit ordering. Where the novels establish causality, they take precedence. Where albums, videos, and demonstrations expand the setting without fixing their exact position, this chapter states the uncertainty instead of forcing a false sequence. In particular, the relationship between all events represented by *DIVISIONS* and *HORIZONS* remains only partially fixed by the currently registered sources.

This is the narrative spine of **The STARSET Codex**. Later chapters will separate the events summarized here into detailed studies of technologies, organizations, characters, locations, adaptations, demonstrations, and unresolved contradictions.

## 2.1 How to Read the Chronology

The STARSET story is not best understood as five albums occurring one after another because the albums were released one after another. The primary narrative introduces information moving backward through time, attempts to prevent a catastrophic future, and a later world that is recognizably different from the future the first warning described. The safest reconstruction is therefore causal rather than merely release-based. [CIT-0004] [CIT-0005]

For this chapter, the chronology is divided into three broad historical strata:

1. **The warned future** — the future history that motivated the PROX Transmission and the attempt to change the past.
2. **The intervention era** — the historical and near-present reception of the warning, the conflict around it, and the effort to prevent that future.
3. **The BMI-era future** — the later dystopia organized around the Brain Machine Interface, New East, New West, the Architecture, rebellion, and the narrative frontier leading into *SILOS*.

Calling these strata separate “timelines” is useful shorthand, but the exact metaphysics of whether history branches, overwrites itself, or behaves according to another model will be treated in the dedicated temporal-mechanics chapter. The primary point established here is simpler: information from a catastrophic future is deliberately used to alter the conditions that produced it. [CIT-0004]

## 2.2 The Warned Future: Technology Becomes a System of Power

**EVT-0001 — The rise of the Everything Machine future**

The first great catastrophe in the STARSET narrative does not begin with an alien invasion or a conventional apocalypse. It begins with human technology becoming economically and politically transformative faster than society can safely absorb it. In the future history carried by the PROX material, the Everything Machine becomes one of the decisive technologies around which power concentrates. [CIT-0006]

The Everything Machine is not important merely because it can manufacture extraordinary things. Its importance is structural. A technology capable of radically changing production also changes who needs labor, who controls scarcity, who owns infrastructure, and which institutions remain necessary. The future described by the warning is therefore a technological dystopia built through incentives and concentration of control rather than a sudden act of supernatural evil. [CIT-0006]

The future history associates this transformation with the Free Energy Corporation and a broader consolidation of industrial power. The exact sequence, dates, and institutional labels require page-level verification against the primary novel before the chapter leaves Draft status, but the causal shape is clear: advanced technology produces enormous leverage; that leverage becomes concentrated; and the resulting order reduces human autonomy rather than expanding it. [CIT-0006]

This distinction matters because it establishes the recurring pattern of the entire saga. The danger is not that technology is inherently malevolent. The danger is that a genuinely useful technology can become the infrastructure through which a small number of actors control everyone else.

## 2.3 PROX and the Escape from Earth

**EVT-0002 — PROX becomes central to humanity's future**

The warned future extends beyond Earth. The first novel and the wider *Transmissions*/*Vessels* material connect the crisis to PROX, an extrasolar destination associated with human expansion and with the origin of the warning that reaches the past. [CIT-0007] [CIT-0008]

PROX is important for two reasons. First, it proves that the technological future is not geographically confined to Earth. Human beings carry their political systems, ambitions, and conflicts with them into space. Second, PROX becomes the vantage point from which the catastrophe can be seen historically. By the time the warning is sent, the problem is no longer a hypothetical policy mistake; it is a lived history whose consequences justify the extraordinary attempt to interfere with the past. [CIT-0007]

*Vessels* later returns explicitly to PROX-facing themes. Official release material describes the album as moving from a “return to Prox” into warnings involving genetic engineering and artificial intelligence. That makes *Vessels* valuable evidence for the continued importance of the original future's technological questions, even where the album does not provide a clean event-by-event chronology. [CIT-0008]

## 2.4 Thomas Bell Sends the Warning

**EVT-0003 — The PROX Transmission is sent backward**

At the center of the first narrative is Thomas Bell, the future figure associated with the information that becomes known as the PROX Transmission. The transmitted material contains more than a simple plea for help. It communicates a warning about the future and advanced technological information whose existence makes the message both potentially salvific and catastrophically valuable to anyone seeking power. [CIT-0009]

The sender's problem is inherently paradoxical. If the warning succeeds, the history that caused the warning may never occur in the same way. Yet doing nothing guarantees that the known catastrophe remains unchallenged. The act of transmission is therefore a deliberate wager: knowledge of the future is sent into the past in the hope that people there will use it to prevent the conditions from which the message originated. [CIT-0009]

This act creates the foundational conflict of *The Prox Transmissions*. The message can save humanity only if it is understood and used responsibly. The same data can also accelerate the very technologies and concentrations of power it was intended to prevent. Possession of the warning therefore becomes almost as dangerous as ignorance of it.

## 2.5 The Earliest Known Reception: Tesla and the Hidden War

**EVT-0004 — The warning reaches an earlier technological age**

The *Transmissions* era deliberately overlays its science fiction onto real technological history. Nikola Tesla is incorporated into the narrative as an early recipient or investigator of the future transmission, linking the fictional warning to the real-world mythology surrounding wireless communication, experimental power systems, and technologies that seemed ahead of their era. [CIT-0010]

The important distinction is that Tesla himself is historical; the role assigned to him in receiving a message from the future is fictional. The Codex will maintain this separation in every later character and location entry.

Around the warning grows a hidden conflict between groups that do not agree on what should be done with the knowledge. The Order of Teslonia represents the protective side of that conflict: knowledge of dangerous future technology must be preserved, understood, and kept from actors likely to reproduce the warned catastrophe. Opposing interests seek control of the same information for power and technological advantage. [CIT-0011]

The first novel presents this conflict as older than Stephen Browning's involvement. Browning does not discover a clean scientific mystery waiting untouched for him. He enters a struggle with history, factions, secrets, and previous attempts to control the transmission already behind it.

## 2.6 Stephen Browning Loses Time

**EVT-0005 — Browning awakens with a missing month**

The near-present story of *The Prox Transmissions* begins not with a triumphant discovery but with disorientation. SETI radio astronomer Stephen Browning wakes in a gas-station bathroom with no clear understanding of how he arrived there. When he attempts to return to his normal life, he discovers that more time has passed than he remembers and that the world has continued without him. [CIT-0012]

A letter written in his own handwriting instructs him to shave his head. Beneath his hair he finds coordinates tattooed onto his scalp. The coordinates become the first reliable thread through the gap in his memory, eventually leading him toward the signal and the hidden work his missing self had already begun. [CIT-0012]

This opening establishes one of the most important narrative facts about Browning: by the time his conscious investigation begins, he is already involved. The mystery is not simply “What is the signal?” but also “What did I learn, what did I do, and why was it necessary that I forget?”

The missing time turns Browning into both investigator and evidence. He cannot initially trust his own continuity, yet the physical clues left by his earlier actions show that he expected his later self to continue the work.

## 2.7 The Signal from Barnard's Star and the Search for Meaning

**EVT-0006 — Browning recovers the future warning**

Following the trail left during his lost period, Browning reaches the information that draws the scientific mystery into the open: a cryptic signal originating from far outside the Solar System, associated with Barnard's Star and PROX. [CIT-0013]

The signal is unmistakably more than ordinary astronomical noise. It contains structured information, advanced technological implications, and a warning whose origin lies in humanity's future rather than an unrelated extraterrestrial civilization. Once that becomes apparent, the stakes change completely. Browning is no longer dealing with evidence that intelligent life exists somewhere else; he is dealing with evidence that human history itself has become a communications channel. [CIT-0013]

The distinction explains why the signal attracts more than scientists. A message from another civilization might transform philosophy. A technically actionable message from the future can transform markets, governments, weapons, infrastructure, and the distribution of power immediately.

## 2.8 Aston Wise and Dana Milano Join the Investigation

**EVT-0007 — The investigation becomes a team**

Browning cannot decode or protect the material alone. He turns to Aston Wise, a wealthy and exceptionally capable engineer, and the investigation expands further with NASA robotics programmer Dana Milano. Together they provide the technical range required to approach the transmission as more than a radio-astronomy anomaly. [CIT-0014]

Their collaboration is not frictionless. *The Prox Transmissions* is built around secrecy as much as science: characters hold different pieces of the truth, factional loyalties are not immediately obvious, and knowledge itself creates reasons for betrayal. The team therefore spends much of the story trying to determine not only what the transmission means but who can safely know that meaning. [CIT-0014]

The transmission's advanced information creates the central strategic dilemma. Destroying it could protect the present from dangerous technology but also erase the warning. Hoarding it invites the same concentration of power that helped produce the catastrophe. Releasing it risks giving hostile actors the means to accelerate the future they are trying to prevent.

## 2.9 The Order, the Collective, and the Fight over the Future

**EVT-0008 — Competing factions move openly around the transmission**

As Browning's investigation deepens, the conflict resolves into competing organizations with different relationships to the future knowledge. The Order of Teslonia seeks to prevent the transmission from being exploited in ways that recreate the catastrophe. Opposing industrial interests—the Collective and associated power structures represented in the first narrative—seek possession of the information for their own purposes. [CIT-0015]

USCOO is part of the coercive machinery Browning and his allies must navigate. The importance of these organizations is not merely that one side chases the protagonists. They embody two incompatible answers to the same question: when knowledge can reorganize civilization, who gets to decide how it is used? [CIT-0015]

The story's conspiracy therefore mirrors the future it is trying to prevent. The future is dominated because transformational technology becomes centralized. In the present, the struggle over the transmission immediately produces actors attempting to centralize transformational knowledge again.

## 2.10 The Warning Changes History

**EVT-0009 — Intervention prevents a simple repetition of the warned future**

The first narrative is an intervention story. Browning, Wise, Milano, and the forces around them act because the future described by the transmission is not accepted as inevitable. The point of receiving the warning is to change the causal chain that produced it. [CIT-0016]

The later canon demonstrates that this effort matters—but it does not reward humanity with a utopia. The future encountered in *A Brief History of the Future* is organized around a different system of technological control: the Brain Machine Interface, New East, New West, and the Architecture. [CIT-0017]

This is the safest point at which to speak of a changed history. The Codex does **not** currently claim that the FEC future vanished into a clean alternate universe or that every person and event was replaced at one instant. What the sources establish is that future-directed information was used to alter history and that the later primary-canon dystopia is materially different from the catastrophe that motivated the first warning. The exact temporal mechanism remains a reconstruction problem for a later chapter. [CIT-0016] [CIT-0017]

## 2.11 *Vessels*: The Questions Survive the Intervention

**EVT-0010 — The original technological warnings broaden**

*Vessels* does not function like a second prose installment. It is better treated as an anthology of perspectives and warnings that expand the technological world introduced by *Transmissions*. Official material explicitly describes a return to Prox, dangers surrounding genetic engineering, and a near future in which artificial intelligence challenges ordinary assumptions about love, life, and death. [CIT-0018]

For the Master Chronology, this means *Vessels* cannot responsibly be converted into fifteen exact dated events. Its significance is thematic and connective: changing one disastrous future does not remove the underlying human tendency to surrender agency to powerful technologies. The particular machine can change while the social vulnerability remains.

This is the bridge into the BMI era. The first saga warns about control over matter and production; the next warns about control over perception, memory, attention, and thought.

## 2.12 The Brain Machine Interface Becomes Ordinary

**EVT-0011 — The BMI transforms daily life**

In the future established by *A Brief History of the Future*, the Brain Machine Interface was not introduced to the public as an instrument of oppression. It was celebrated as access to an augmented world. Its appeal is precisely what makes the system durable. People are not initially dragged into an obviously evil machine; they are offered a technology that makes ordinary reality easier to escape, customize, and consume. [CIT-0019]

The New West becomes saturated with this interface. Artificial experiences provide distraction from poverty and from the political-economic conditions under which New West citizens work for New East managers. The BMI's role is therefore both personal and systemic: it alters what an individual experiences while making the structure around that individual easier to tolerate. [CIT-0019]

The result is a form of control more intimate than the Everything Machine future. The previous dystopia can dominate what people are able to own, produce, or access. The BMI can mediate what they perceive in the first place.

## 2.13 The Architecture Behind the Experience

**EVT-0012 — The Architecture becomes the hidden substrate of the BMI world**

The augmented experiences available through the BMI depend on a larger structure: the Architecture. To ordinary users, the interface can appear as entertainment, utility, escape, or social infrastructure. Behind those experiences lies the system that enables and controls them. [CIT-0020]

This is where the BMI future stops being merely a story about addictive technology. If a platform mediates an entire population's artificial reality, whoever controls the platform has power over more than software. It has leverage over attention, memory, behavior, social meaning, and the boundary between experienced reality and physical reality.

The Architecture is therefore the institutional equivalent of the central problem established in the first novel: transformative capability becomes dangerous when the system around it allows control to concentrate.

## 2.14 Thomas Bell in the BMI Future

**EVT-0013 — Thomas enters the Architecture**

The Thomas Bell of *A Brief History of the Future* is a graduate student studying advanced programming. His personal ambitions are shaped by loss and economic pressure rather than abstract revolution. His father died after pursuing rebellious ideals, and Thomas has learned to distrust heroic gestures that leave surviving family members to absorb the consequences. His mother requires medical treatment, and Thomas needs money. [CIT-0021]

To pay for that care, he subjects himself to virtual torture for cash. The detail is grotesque precisely because it demonstrates how normalized artificial experience has become: even pain can be converted into a market, and a human nervous system can become a rentable resource when the underlying technology is sufficiently advanced. [CIT-0021]

Thomas is then selected for an elite accelerated internship inside the Architecture. At first, this appears to be the solution to his problems. It validates his intelligence, offers substantial financial reward, and gives him access to the most important technical system in his society. [CIT-0021]

But access changes the meaning of the opportunity. Once inside, he discovers that he has dedicated his abilities to a structure far larger and more sinister than he understood from outside. The central question of the second novel is no longer whether Thomas can obtain a future for himself and his mother. It is whether any private future remains possible once he understands the system he is helping to build. [CIT-0021]

## 2.15 *DIVISIONS*: Control Becomes Visible

**EVT-0014 — The New East order becomes openly coercive**

*DIVISIONS* presents the BMI world after the logic of the system has become difficult to disguise. New East propaganda speaks in the language of unity, allegiance, acceptable thought, and productive citizenship. The official visual narrative for “WHERE THE SKIES END” makes the threat explicit: contraband thought and contraband ideas are treated as offenses, and offenders face supplication. [CIT-0022]

Supplication is more than imprisonment. BMI technology makes it possible to separate physical presence from experienced autonomy. A body can remain in the world while the person's subjective experience is constrained by the system. In “WHERE THE SKIES END,” the apparent facility is revealed to depend on BMI-mediated perception; when the interface fails, the distinction between the imposed environment and physical reality becomes visible. [CIT-0022]

The regime's greatest weapon is therefore not simply surveillance. It is the ability to make control feel like reality itself.

At the same time, *DIVISIONS* is not a story of total ideological victory. Rebel imagery, anti-Architecture messaging, and resistance language persist throughout the era. The existence of propaganda reveals the existence of an audience that still has to be persuaded, threatened, distracted, or suppressed.

## 2.16 *HORIZONS*: Breach, Escape, and the Problem of Ordering

**EVT-0015 — Resistance to BMI control remains active**

*HORIZONS* continues the technological and political concerns of the BMI era, but its exact position relative to every event represented by *DIVISIONS* is not explicitly fixed by the sources currently registered in the Codex. The album should therefore not be flattened into “before *DIVISIONS*” or “after *DIVISIONS*” as a universal rule. [CIT-0023]

The official video for “THE BREACH” is nevertheless clear about the world it depicts: BMI-era control exists, family and community life are shaped by the system, and escape from the controlled environment is both materially dangerous and psychologically difficult. [CIT-0023]

What *HORIZONS* adds to the chronology is not a clean calendar date but a widening horizon of resistance. The BMI is established enough to structure society, yet people can still cross boundaries, reject imposed reality, and search for something outside the system.

The uncertainty itself is canonically important. STARSET's albums often function as windows into conditions, perspectives, and warnings rather than as consecutively numbered chapters of one prose narrative. The Codex will place individual *HORIZONS* events more precisely only where later evidence justifies doing so.

## 2.17 *IMMERSION: THE FINAL CHAPTER*: The Dystopia Becomes a Dramatic Narrative

**EVT-0016 — The 2024 demonstrations stage the rebellion conflict directly**

The 2024 *IMMERSION: THE FINAL CHAPTER* demonstrations were explicitly promoted as a dystopian futuristic narrative intended to bring anticipated conclusions to threads developed across STARSET's multimedia story. They therefore occupy an unusually important position between the second novel, the BMI-era albums, and the material that followed. [CIT-0024]

The demonstration's cinematic story follows an Auditor whose place inside the system becomes personal when his wife is supplicated. It also introduces John Handworth, whose technical work becomes tied to suppressing or interrupting the BMI signal, and a rebellion seeking to use such knowledge against the controlled order. [CIT-0024]

The Auditor's role is important because he begins on the side of institutional enforcement rather than outside it. The system ceases to be an abstraction when its mechanisms are applied to his own home. His choices then become dangerous in both directions: obedience preserves the institution that destroyed his wife's autonomy, while disobedience exposes him to that same institution.

Handworth's work attacks the regime at the layer where it is strongest. If BMI control depends on a continuous technical signal, then interrupting that signal—even temporarily—creates the possibility of unmediated perception and autonomous action. This turns resistance from ideology into an engineering problem. [CIT-0024]

The demonstration culminates in consequences for the Auditor himself. The story does not present the New East system as a bureaucracy that tolerates conscience indefinitely; enforcement eventually reaches the enforcer. [CIT-0024]

## 2.18 The Messenger Problem Returns

**EVT-0017 — Warning the past remains central to the saga's identity**

Across both major dystopian phases, STARSET repeatedly returns to the figure of the messenger: someone who possesses knowledge that matters only if another person receives it in time. In the first saga, the message literally crosses time. In the BMI era, rebellion depends on breaking through layers of technological mediation so that people can perceive the structure controlling them.

This recurrence is not enough, by itself, to prove that every “messenger” reference identifies the same person or the same mechanism. It does establish a thematic and narrative continuity: the problem is never merely knowing the truth. The problem is transmitting it through systems designed—deliberately or structurally—to prevent it from changing behavior. [CIT-0025]

## 2.19 *SILOS*: Return, Fragmentation, and the Current Narrative Frontier

**EVT-0018 — The fifth album reopens foundational threads**

*SILOS*, STARSET's fifth studio album, was released on January 9, 2026. Its track sequence combines titles associated with the recent dystopian era—“DEGENERATE,” “BRAVE NEW WORLD,” “DYSTOPIA,” and “TOKSIK”—with titles that explicitly invoke older foundational concepts: “rise of messenger,” “temple of milton,” “AD ASTRA,” and “requiem of the order.” [CIT-0026]

This matters because *SILOS* does not read like a clean abandonment of the earlier PROX/Order narrative in favor of an unrelated new setting. Even at the level of explicit titles and official visual branding, the album brings Order- and PROX-facing language back into the same release that contains the modern dystopian singles. Official merchandise for the era even includes a PROX blueprint poster alongside New West and rebel propaganda designs, reinforcing the coexistence of those narrative vocabularies. [CIT-0026]

The temptation is to convert that coexistence immediately into a definitive plot claim: that the two historical strata have merged, that the Order has returned in a particular form, or that a specific character has become the new messenger. The currently registered evidence does not yet justify those conclusions.

What can be stated safely is that *SILOS* is the present endpoint of the Codex's initial chronology and that it deliberately reactivates names and symbols from the saga's foundations while continuing themes of manipulation, social degradation, dystopia, devotion, rebellion, and transmission. The album's final title, “requiem of the order,” makes the Order impossible to ignore, but a requiem can signify death, memorial, transformation, or narrative closure; the title alone does not tell us which. [CIT-0026]

Accordingly, this first edition of the Master Chronology ends not with a fabricated resolution but with an open frontier. The warned future was challenged. A different technological order emerged. The BMI became a mechanism of perception and control. Resistance learned that breaking the system required both technical knowledge and human willingness to act. And by *SILOS*, the language of PROX, messengers, and the Order has returned to the surface of the story.

What that return ultimately means is one of the questions the next canonical release will have the authority to answer.

## 2.21 What This Chronology Does Not Claim

Several popular simplifications are deliberately **not** adopted as canon in this chapter:

- The Codex does not claim that every album is a single uninterrupted sequence of literal events.
- The Codex does not claim that *HORIZONS* universally precedes *DIVISIONS* or universally follows it.
- The Codex does not yet claim a specific branching-versus-overwriting model for altered history.
- The Codex does not claim that every Thomas Bell reference across changed histories can be understood without qualification as one uninterrupted biography.
- The Codex does not infer the ultimate fate of the Order solely from the title “requiem of the order.”
- The Codex does not infer that the word “messenger” always refers to one specific individual.
- The Codex does not treat community reconstructions of exact dates as primary evidence until those dates are verified in official source material.

These are not omissions. They are boundaries around what the current evidence can actually support.

## Source Notes

This chapter intentionally mixes different levels of chronological precision.

The two novels are Tier I sources and establish the principal causal spine. Several primary-text claims currently use work-level rather than page-level locators because page-specific verification has not yet been completed in the repository. Those citations are explicitly marked for verification in `sources.yaml` and must be upgraded before a final public release.

Albums are Tier II narrative media. Where their contribution is thematic or environmental rather than a prose-like dated event, the chronology says so. *Vessels* is therefore treated as an expansion of technological themes rather than converted into a fictitious sequence of fifteen events.

The *DIVISIONS* and *HORIZONS* ordering remains deliberately unresolved. The current evidence establishes a shared BMI-era context and specific depicted conditions, but not a universal release-to-chronology mapping.

The *IMMERSION: THE FINAL CHAPTER* demonstration is registered as Tier II official narrative media. Its scene-level reconstruction should be checked against authenticated recordings before publication; this draft does not treat third-party concert summaries as substitutes for the demonstration itself.

*SILOS* is treated as the current narrative frontier. The chapter distinguishes explicit album titles and official era branding from interpretations of what those titles ultimately mean.

## Canon Confidence Summary

This chapter contains all four confidence categories.

- **Confirmed Canon:** the existence and broad function of the PROX warning; Browning's investigation; Wise and Milano's involvement; the BMI/New East/New West/Architecture setting; Thomas Bell's circumstances in the second novel; New East suppression and supplication; the registered album and video content; *SILOS*' explicit track naming.
- **Strongly Supported:** Tesla's precise place in the earliest reception narrative as summarized here; some organization terminology and the scene-level reconstruction of *IMMERSION: THE FINAL CHAPTER* pending direct locator verification.
- **Reasonable Reconstruction:** the three-strata model used to make the chronology readable; the claim that the messenger problem forms a structural continuity across both dystopian phases; the description of *Vessels* as a thematic bridge rather than a dated sequence.
- **Community Theory:** no community theory is adopted as a chronological fact. Popular claims about exact timeline branching, absolute *DIVISIONS*/*HORIZONS* ordering, and the final meaning of *SILOS* are preserved as unresolved rather than promoted into canon.
