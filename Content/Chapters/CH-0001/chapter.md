## Abstract

**The STARSET Codex** is a living, open-source reference project dedicated to documenting the narrative universe associated with STARSET. This chapter establishes the purpose, scope, reading model, evidence standards, and archival framing used throughout the Codex. It is designed to stand on its own as an orientation document: a reader should be able to begin here without prior knowledge of the project, its source hierarchy, or its internal terminology.

The Codex treats lore as evidence to be documented rather than a puzzle to be solved at any cost. Official material is recorded first; interpretation follows only when the evidence requires it. Contradictions are preserved rather than erased, uncertainty is labeled rather than hidden, and later revisions remain traceable to the sources and editorial decisions that produced them.

## 1.1 Purpose of the Codex

STARSET's narrative is not confined to a single work. The registered foundation of this project already spans the novel *The Prox Transmissions*, its later continuation *A Brief History of the Future*, and the album *Transmissions*. [CIT-0001] [CIT-0002] [CIT-0003] The wider Codex is intended to expand that record across every lore-bearing official medium that can be authenticated and documented under the project's editorial standards.

The purpose of the Codex is therefore not merely to summarize a story. It is to create a durable research archive in which readers can determine what happened, why it happened, how different sources describe it, where uncertainty begins, and which evidence supports each conclusion.

This produces two complementary ways to use the project. A reader may approach the Codex as a continuous history of the STARSET universe, beginning with the Master Chronology in CH-0002, or as a reference work in which individual chapters document characters, organizations, technologies, locations, works, events, and unresolved questions in greater depth.

## 1.2 Scope

The initial content release of **The STARSET Codex** is intended to document the available narrative through *SILOS*. The project scope includes, where relevant and verifiable:

- Novels and officially published prose.
- Albums and songs with narrative relevance.
- Official music videos.
- Comic adaptations.
- Live demonstrations and narrative material presented through them.
- Alternate reality games and associated recovered material.
- Official websites and archived web material.
- Creator commentary when it materially clarifies the published narrative.
- Historical facts required to distinguish real people, places, technologies, and events from fictional additions.
- Community research when it documents discovery history, preservation, or interpretation and is clearly distinguished from official evidence.

Inclusion in the Codex does not automatically grant a source equal canonical authority. Source authority is governed by the Editorial Policy, while the strength of individual conclusions is governed by the Canon Confidence specification.

## 1.3 How the Codex Is Organized

The Codex is deliberately structured so that narrative reading and reference research can coexist.

**Chapter 1** provides orientation. **Chapter 2, the Master Chronology**, presents the known history as a continuous chronological account, allowing a reader to experience the major sequence of events before entering the deeper reference material. Later chapters then expand the people, organizations, technologies, locations, works, and historical periods introduced by that chronology.

Cross-references use permanent identifiers rather than relative descriptions. A reference such as `CH-0002` will continue to identify the Master Chronology even if the repository later changes its folder structure, generated website routes, or PDF pagination.

Each chapter is also independently publishable. Essential context cannot be outsourced to another chapter merely to avoid repetition. Cross-references provide depth, not missing foundations.

## 1.4 Evidence Before Interpretation

The central editorial principle of the Codex is simple:

> **Document first. Interpret second.**

When an official source explicitly establishes an event, identity, relationship, date, location, or mechanism, the Codex records that evidence before discussing what it may imply. When the available material does not establish a conclusion directly, the conclusion is labeled according to the evidence required to reach it.

This distinction matters because STARSET's narrative frequently invites interpretation. A compelling theory can explain several clues and still remain a theory. Conversely, an inconvenient contradiction does not disappear because one interpretation produces a cleaner timeline.

The Codex therefore prefers an incomplete but honest record over a complete narrative built from undocumented assumptions.

## 1.5 Canon Confidence

The Codex uses four confidence levels defined by `DOC-SPEC-0002`:

- **Confirmed Canon** — explicitly established by official material.
- **Strongly Supported** — supported by multiple official pieces of evidence without being directly stated as a single conclusion.
- **Reasonable Reconstruction** — an evidence-based inference required to connect incomplete information.
- **Community Theory** — an interpretation that remains insufficiently supported for a higher confidence level.

Confidence describes the support for a claim; it does not replace source authority. A claim may involve a highly authoritative source and still require inference. Likewise, a lower-tier source may explicitly establish what that particular source depicts while remaining subordinate to higher-tier material when a contradiction exists.

Readers should therefore treat confidence labels as an explanation of *how far the evidence reaches*, not as a score for how interesting or persuasive an interpretation may be.

## 1.6 Contradictions and Changing Lore

The Codex follows a **No Lore Left Behind** policy. When two sources conflict, the older account is not silently rewritten to match the newer one. Both accounts are preserved, their sources are identified, and the conflict is analyzed according to the Editorial Policy.

This distinction is especially important in a project concerned with altered histories, unreliable information, adaptations, and material released across different media. A contradiction may ultimately prove to be an intentional part of the fiction, an adaptation difference, a retcon, an error, or an unresolved question. Unless the evidence establishes which explanation applies, the Codex does not choose one merely because it produces the neatest result.

Superseded material remains historically useful even when it no longer represents the highest-authority account. The Codex documents the development of the lore as well as its current interpretation.

## 1.7 Historical People and Fictional Characters

Where STARSET incorporates real people, places, scientific concepts, institutions, or historical events into its fiction, the Codex separates documented history from fictional narrative additions.

Character and organization material will identify whether a person is historical or fictional. For historical figures, relevant chapters will distinguish three layers whenever the evidence allows it:

1. The documented historical record.
2. The role assigned to that person by STARSET's fiction.
3. Any reconstruction used by the Codex to connect the two.

The same principle applies to real-world locations. A real place may be confirmed as historically existing while its secret function within the STARSET narrative remains fictional, inferred, or uncertain.

## 1.8 Maps, Diagrams, Figures, and Reconstructions

Visual material in the Codex is evidence-conscious. Maps, diagrams, and figures use separate permanent identifier families so that a geographic reconstruction cannot be mistaken for a photograph or a conceptual diagram.

Maps may combine confirmed locations with inferred positions, but the distinction must be visible. Diagrams may simplify complex relationships for readability, but they cannot convert uncertainty into certainty. Reconstructed imagery must identify its creator, source basis, revision, and confidence where applicable.

This is particularly important for real-world mapping. A location described only approximately in narrative material will not be presented as an exact point simply because modern mapping tools make such precision possible.

## 1.9 The Archival Frame

The Codex uses the presentation language of a recovered STARSET Society archive. Chapter headers, Archivist's Notes, recovered-document inserts, classifications, authentication fields, and similar devices create an in-universe archival identity for the project.

That framing is presentation, not evidence.

An Archivist's Note may connect citations, explain a discrepancy, or make a difficult section easier to navigate. It may not invent a recovered conversation, classified operation, technical specification, or historical fact and then use the fictional presentation as justification for treating it as canon.

When the Codex creates an immersive document that did not originate in official STARSET material, it must remain identifiable as an editorial reconstruction or original presentation asset.

> **Archivist's Note — ARC-0001**  
> An archive is useful only when the reader can tell the difference between the record and the archivist. The Codex may adopt the voice of the STARSET Society, but its evidence must remain visible outside that voice.

## 1.10 A Living, Versioned Archive

The STARSET Codex is designed to survive new releases, recovered material, corrections, and future community maintenance. Permanent identifiers allow objects to remain addressable across revisions. Registries preserve the history of sources and project objects. Published releases are immutable: corrections are issued in later versions rather than silently altering an existing public release.

Markdown is the canonical human-readable source for chapters. Machine-readable metadata remains in companion YAML files. Generated PDFs, and later the project website and other presentation formats, are outputs built from that maintained source rather than competing versions of the lore.

This architecture allows a future contributor to revise one chapter without rewriting unrelated material, while still making the effects of that revision traceable across citations, registries, chronology, and generated outputs.

## 1.11 Reading Forward

Readers who want the story first should continue directly to **CH-0002, Master Chronology**. It will assemble the major known events into one chronological narrative before the Codex separates them into specialized reference chapters.

Readers using the project as a research archive may instead move directly to the relevant subject chapter and use its cross-references, citations, confidence labels, and source notes to follow the evidence outward.

Both paths serve the same goal: preserving a version of the STARSET narrative in which the boundary between what is known, what is inferred, and what remains unresolved is always visible.
